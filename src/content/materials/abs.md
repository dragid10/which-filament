---
title: ABS
fullName: Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene
summary: Tough and heat-resistant, and sands and smooths well. Needs an enclosure and good ventilation.
order: 3
homeUse: care
homeNote: "Needs an enclosed printer, and a ventilated space or vented enclosure: it gives off styrene fumes."
pros:
  - "Heat deflection around 95-100 °C, well above PLA and PETG"
  - "Tough and impact-resistant, and not brittle"
  - "Sands, drills, and glues easily; acetone vapor smoothing gives a glossy finish"
  - "Cheap compared with engineering filaments"
cons:
  - "Warps and cracks without an enclosure, especially on large parts"
  - "Gives off fumes while printing (styrene). Vent the enclosure to the outdoors; a HEPA filter alone does not remove the gases"
  - "Degrades in sunlight (yellows and gets brittle); use ASA for outdoors"
  - "Harder to print than PLA or PETG"
settings:
  nozzle: 230-260 °C
  bed: 90-110 °C
  enclosure: Required (a warm chamber of roughly 40-55 °C helps prevent warping)
  drying: 70-80 °C for 4-6 h
sources:
  - title: "PrintPal: ABS and ASA guide"
    url: "https://printpal.io/wiki/abs-asa-complete-guide"
  - title: "All3DP: ASA vs ABS"
    url: "https://all3dp.com/2/asa-vs-abs-differences/"
  - title: "Elegoo: filament drying and storage guide"
    url: "https://wiki.elegoo.com/filaments/fdm-filament-drying-and-storage-guide"
---

- The classic engineering plastic for heat and hard use.
- Needs an enclosed printer and ventilation. On an open printer, use PETG.
- Outdoors it yellows and cracks, so use [ASA](/materials/asa) instead.
