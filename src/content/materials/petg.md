---
title: PETG
fullName: Polyethylene terephthalate glycol
summary: The tougher all-rounder. More heat- and impact-resistant than PLA with only a little more fuss.
order: 2
pros:
  - "Tougher and less brittle than PLA; flexes before it breaks"
  - "Handles around 70-80 °C, hot cars included"
  - "Good layer adhesion, so parts hold together well"
  - "Holds up better to moisture and many chemicals"
cons:
  - "Stringing and blobs unless settings are dialled in"
  - "Soaks up moisture from the air, so keep it dry"
  - "Sticks very well to smooth PEI; use a release agent or glue stick to protect the plate"
  - "Scratches more easily and is a bit less crisp on fine detail than PLA"
settings:
  nozzle: 230-250 °C
  bed: 70-85 °C
  enclosure: Optional (helps with large parts and drafts)
  drying: 60-65 °C for 6-8 h
---

PETG is the usual step up when PLA is not tough enough. It is the go-to for functional parts, anything that sees sun or warmth, and parts that need to survive being dropped.

It asks for slightly more care: hotter temperatures, lower part cooling, and dry filament. Once tuned it is nearly as easy as PLA.

Like PLA it comes in variants. **Rapid PETG** is the high-speed version; standard PETG is the safe default.
