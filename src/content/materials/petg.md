---
title: PETG
fullName: Glycol-modified polyethylene terephthalate
summary: The tougher all-rounder. More heat- and impact-resistant than PLA with only a little more fuss.
order: 2
homeUse: easy
homeNote: "Works on most FDM printers. An enclosure is optional."
aliases: [PET-G, PETG]
pros:
  - "Tougher and less brittle than PLA; flexes before it breaks"
  - "Softens around 75-85 °C, a step above PLA (handles most car interiors)"
  - "Good layer adhesion, so parts hold together well"
  - "Printed parts resist water and many chemicals better than PLA"
cons:
  - "Stringing and blobs unless settings are dialed in"
  - "Soaks up moisture from the air, so keep it dry"
  - "Sticks very well to smooth PEI; use a release agent or glue stick to protect the plate"
  - "Scratches more easily and is a bit less crisp on fine detail than PLA"
  - "Not UV-stable for years in full sun: it can yellow and weaken over months to years, so use ASA for that"
settings:
  nozzle: 230-250 °C
  bed: 70-85 °C
  enclosure: Optional (helps with large parts and drafts)
  drying: 60-65 °C for 6-8 h

- The usual step up when PLA is not tough enough: functional parts, warm spots, drops, and outdoor use in the shade.
- A bit fussier than PLA: hotter, less part cooling, and dry filament.
- Plain PETG is the safe default; Rapid PETG is the high-speed version.
